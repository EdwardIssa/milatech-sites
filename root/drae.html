Products · ML Safety

DRAE — when your models disagree, something should stop.

Most production ML fails quietly. The ensemble still answers, the API still returns 200, and the first sign of trouble is a customer, an auditor, or a regulator finding the bad output before you do. DRAE is a runtime governance layer that sits between your models and the world: it measures how much your models disagree with each other on every live decision, blocks outputs that cross the lines you set — fail-closed, never fail-silent — and governs what your autonomous agents are permitted to do. It is governance that runs in the request path, not in a quarterly review.

The problem

Ensembles hide their own failure

An ensemble exists because no single model is trustworthy everywhere. But averaging models together also averages away the most useful danger signal you have: the moments when they sharply disagree. A confident average can conceal a coin-flip underneath — two models near-certain of opposite answers produce the same bland score as two models in calm agreement. Standard monitoring watches inputs and outputs; it rarely watches the spread between the models themselves, so drift, edge cases, and adversarial inputs all present the same way: as normal traffic, until they don’t. And when teams bolt autonomous agents onto those models, the blast radius grows — an agent doesn’t just answer, it acts.

How DRAE works

Three mechanisms, one runtime layer

Measure

ω-Disagreement monitoring

On every decision, DRAE computes a live disagreement score across your ensemble’s members — not a nightly batch job, but a per-request signal you can alert on, log, and route by. Rising disagreement is the earliest honest indicator that the world has moved away from your training data: new inputs, new behavior, new failure modes. Instead of discovering drift from a complaint, you watch it arrive, with the exact decisions that carried it attached.

Stop

Fail-closed Policy Interlock

You define the lines: disagreement ceilings, confidence floors, output policies per model, per route, per customer tier. When a live decision crosses one, the Interlock blocks it — the request fails closed to a safe default, a human queue, or a fallback model, and the block itself is logged with its reasons. Nothing degrades silently into production. The interlock is deliberately boring technology: explicit rules, evaluated in-path, with an audit trail your compliance team can actually read.

Govern

Agent Governor

Autonomous agents get the same treatment as models, at the action layer. The Governor holds each agent’s permissions — which tools it may call, which actions require the interlock’s approval, which are never allowed — and enforces them at runtime rather than trusting the prompt. An agent that tries to step outside its mandate is stopped and logged, not scolded. Autonomy becomes something you grant in measured amounts, with evidence, instead of something you hope holds.

Evidence

Benchmarked, tested, inspectable

DRAE ships with a reproducible benchmark (fixed seed, published methodology) that measures how the governance layer behaves when ensemble members are pushed into disagreement — what gets blocked, what gets through, and what it costs in latency. The current release, v1.1.1, passes its full test suite (22 of 22) and carries a proprietary Mila Tech LLC license. We would rather show you a benchmark you can re-run than a testimonial you can’t. The full walkthrough video, architecture notes, and benchmark details live on the DRAE site.

Who it’s for

Teams whose models make decisions that matter

DRAE is built for ML platform teams running ensembles in production, for organizations in regulated industries — finance, insurance, healthcare — where “the model said so” is not an acceptable audit answer, and for teams putting autonomous agents to work who need the agent’s authority written down and enforced somewhere other than its system prompt. If a wrong model output costs you money, customers, or a conversation with a regulator, DRAE is the layer that makes sure you hear about it first — from your own systems, in time to act.
